## 1. Project Overview

The Coastal Agriculture Research Project investigates the feasibility of establishing sustainable agricultural operations in coastal regions of South Africa.

The research focuses on soil quality, groundwater availability, seasonal weather patterns, crop selection, and the economic viability of small-scale farming operations.

The initial research area covers approximately 145 hectares of agricultural land situated outside George in the Western Cape.

The project is expected to run for 18 months, with the first six months dedicated to environmental assessments and preliminary research.

## 2. Environmental Findings

Initial soil assessments identified three primary soil classifications:

- Sandy loam: 52% of sampled locations.
- Clay loam: 31% of sampled locations.
- Silty soil: 17% of sampled locations.

The average recorded soil pH was 6.4.

Groundwater samples were collected from four separate locations. The average groundwater temperature was 18.7°C.

Researchers identified inconsistent rainfall distribution as one of the primary challenges affecting agricultural productivity in the region.

## 4. Crop Selection

The research team shortlisted five potential crops based on their suitability for the region.

| Crop | Estimated Yield per Hectare | Growing Period |
|---|---:|---|
| Blueberries | 8,500 kg | 12 months |
| Avocados | 10,000 kg | 36 months |
| Potatoes | 32,000 kg | 4 months |
| Tomatoes | 45,000 kg | 5 months |
| Butternut | 18,000 kg | 5 months |

Potatoes and tomatoes were identified as potential candidates for the first production cycle due to their relatively short growing periods.

Avocados were considered a longer-term investment.
